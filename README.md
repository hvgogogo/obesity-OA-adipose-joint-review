# Adipose–joint crosstalk in obesity-induced osteoarthritis

**Mechanisms, biomarkers and translational therapeutic opportunities**

Published in *Current Rheumatology Reports* 28, 22 (2026) · [doi:10.1007/s11926-026-01229-9](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11926-026-01229-9) · open access (CC BY 4.0)

**Wei Hang** (first author), Kathy Triantafilou, You Zhou · Cardiff University

---

## Why this review

Osteoarthritis (OA) is usually explained by mechanical wear and ageing. But obesity also raises the risk of OA in joints that carry no extra weight, such as the hands. This points to a **metabolic** form of OA, driven by signals from fat tissue rather than by load alone. Current treatments mostly target joint damage after it happens. This review brings together what is known about how fat tissue and the joint talk to each other, and what that means for treatment.

## What it covers

**1. How obesity drives joint damage**
- Different fat depots play different roles: **visceral fat** as a body-wide source of inflammation, **subcutaneous fat** through lipid overflow into the joint, and the **infrapatellar fat pad** as a direct local link to the knee.
- Lipid changes (free fatty acids, oxidised LDL), adipokines (leptin, adiponectin), lipokines, extracellular vesicles and complement.
- How these signals converge on shared pathways in joint tissues, such as NF-κB.

**2. Adipose–joint crosstalk at the cellular level**
- Communication between adipocytes, macrophages, synovial fibroblasts and chondrocytes that keeps low-grade inflammation, oxidative stress and matrix breakdown going.
- **Single-cell and spatial transcriptomic evidence** for macrophage and fibroblast subsets that drive depot-specific inflammatory circuits.
- A timeline of how the joint changes as the disease progresses, and how to recognise the obesity-induced OA phenotype in patients.

**3. Treatment by disease stage**
- **Early:** metabolic intervention, including GLP-1 receptor agonists.
- **Intermediate:** combined targeting of metabolism and inflammation, including anti-adipokine approaches.
- **Advanced:** disease-modifying and regenerative strategies.
- Biomarkers for patient selection and how future clinical trials could be designed.

## Main message

Obesity-induced OA comes from metabolic and inflammatory crosstalk between fat tissue and the joint at several levels. Treating these upstream drivers, rather than only the damaged joint, is the basis for more precise therapies.
